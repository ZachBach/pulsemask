Unit 01 · Generator

Wearable art, generated from one photograph.

Upload a front-facing portrait and mark five landmarks. The system scales an anthropometric face model to your measurements, adds surface relief recovered from the photograph, and grows a sealing shell onto it — then hands you a printable STL in millimetres.

It is a design study in respirator form, and it is sold as art. The geometry is genuinely engineered — real parameters, real physics, real printable output. The air-treatment side of the idea does not yet work, and rather than hide that, the failing number is on this page in three places. Read the mission and the current status.

What this is not: single-image capture cannot recover true metric depth. Landmarks set the proportions; photometric relief adds surface detail. A validated fit needs a depth scan or a plaster cast. Nothing here is a respirator, PPE or a medical device, and nothing here protects you from anything.

Case study

Design notes

Why an inner oronasal cup

A full-face shell with a wraparound visor has a large internal volume. Breathe into it directly and you rebreathe CO₂ and fog the lens. The inner cup isolates the breathing circuit: treated air enters the cup, exhaled air leaves through the cheek valves, and the visor cavity is swept by a small bleed. Dead space drops from roughly a litre to the figure in the readout.

Why SLS nylon, not resin

PA12 is tough where photopolymer is brittle. A resin shell that cracks at the visor aperture during a fall is a failure mode with the user's eyes behind it. SLS also prints the organic shell without supports, so no support scars land on the sealing face — that surface has to be smooth or the bead leaks.

The dose problem

At 85 L/min peak inspiratory flow, air crosses a 28 mm reactor in about 27 ms. No practical LED array delivers a germicidal dose in that window. The honest options are a long folded labyrinth, a blower that decouples flow from breathing, or treating UV-C as a filter-media steriliser between breaths rather than an inline kill stage. The sliders let you find the boundary yourself. Note what the physics says: for a uniformly lit tube the dose reduces to P·η·ρ·r / 2Q — reactor length alone buys nothing unless you add emitters with it, which is why the slider is LED density.

What the PZT array does

Five roles, one driver: acoustic agitation in the reactor, ultrasonic coalescence ahead of the filter to grow sub-micron aerosol into capturable droplets, seal-contact sensing for live fit telemetry, blower assist, and breath energy harvesting at the exhale valves. Capsid disruption by ultrasound is theoretical here — it needs bench validation before it is claimed.

What the horns actually carry

The bore is about 11 mm at the base, so no 18650 and no 14500 fits — it takes a curved pouch cell, and both horns carry one. An antenna sitting on top of a cell gets detuned, so the radio moved to the crest keel: centreline, clear of both cells, long enough to radiate. The keel stops being decoration. Runtime is still the weak number — gating the reactor to inhalation only is what turns an afternoon into a working day.
